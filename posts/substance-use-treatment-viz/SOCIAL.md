```
Substance use disorder affects over 48 million Americans age 12 and older. How many treatment workers are there to help them, and how does that differ from state to state?

Today's work comes from Data 4 The People data storytelling fellow Jameson Mehaffey, a second-year data analytics student at The Ohio State University. He spent the summer building a free, interactive map of substance use treatment in all 50 states and DC, from 2001 to 2024.

The map shows:
• the share of people with a substance use disorder (SUD) in each state
• outpatient treatment facilities per 100,000 residents
• how many people with SUD there are for each treatment worker
• the death rate from drug overdose and alcohol-induced causes, which has climbed considerably across the country over the past two decades

As with any good visualization, it brings up far more questions than answers. The first questions Jameson and the rest of the D4TP team are exploring:

1. Why do states have such large gaps in their SUD population per treatment worker?
2. Why has the death rate surged, without the same rise (outside the survey change) in the SUD prevalence rate?
3. What happens to people living with SUD, and the treatment workers caring for them, when Medicaid work requirements and eligibility checks every six months go into effect? Which states could face the most risk?

We will publish a series of follow-up studies using this tool, starting with fentanyl.

In Jameson's words: "Spreading awareness and helping others are the main purposes of this map."

Find your state at the link in the first comment. If you have questions you would like us to explore, let us know.

If you or someone you know needs help now, call or text 988.

#SubstanceUse #PublicHealth #DataVisualization #DataJournalism
```
