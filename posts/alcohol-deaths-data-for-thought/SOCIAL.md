Alcohol use in America is going down. Right?
The headlines are right. Alcoholism is down. But over the same years, alcohol became far more deadly.

From 2002-2003 to 2018-2019:
• The share of Americans with an alcohol use disorder fell 30%.
• The share who drank in the past month barely changed.
• Alcohol-induced deaths per 100,000 people rose 65%.

I immediately assumed it must be age. America is older than it was 20 years ago. Only partly. After adjusting for age, the death rate still rose 46%. The age-adjusted rate went from 7.1 per 100,000 in 1999 to a peak of 14.4 in 2021, and was 12.1 in 2024.

When I studied the data, I found one pattern that stands out. It’s one I’ve written about before, and it shows up in what Americans buy when they reach for a bottle.
