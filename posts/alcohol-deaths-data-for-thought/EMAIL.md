## 3. Body, part one

```
Alcohol use in America is going down.

Right?

I’ve been a passive consumer of media on this topic, seeing random headlines in passing. Over time, these headlines created that story in my mind that alcohol use in America is in structural decline.

But then I spent some time studying Jameson’s Substance Use Disorder (SUD) map and realized my reckless passive consumption of media on this topic created a flawed story in my mind.

So here is what you need to know. The headlines are right. Alcoholism is down, as measured by the share of Americans with an alcohol use disorder. In fact, it fell 30% in the 16 years before the pandemic. And over the same years, alcohol became far more deadly – killing 65% more people per capita.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
I was shocked when I saw this data, because none of the headlines ever mentioned it. How can the death rate from alcohol be up so much at the same time alcoholism is in decline?

I immediately assumed it must be age. Twenty years ago, America was far younger, with a median age of just 36.1. Now America’s median age is 39.1. So, it’s the cumulative impact of alcohol consumption that is leading to more deaths? Only partly. When you age adjust the data, most of the rise is still there: deaths per capita rose 46% after adjusting for age, compared with 65% before.

In fact, when I studied the data, I found one pattern that stands out. It’s one I’ve written about before, and it shows up in what Americans buy when they reach for a bottle.
```
